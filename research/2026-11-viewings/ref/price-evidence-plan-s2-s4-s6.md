## 2. The viewing

**Kit:** pin damp meter; plug-in socket tester; laser measure; marble (sloping floors); small binoculars (roof); torch.

**Rules:** arrive early and walk the outside alone; photograph everything, film one walk-through; show no enthusiasm; ask agent and seller the same questions and write answers down word for word; ask to see the loft, boiler, consumer unit and stopcock; ask before using the socket tester; don't record the seller's voice without consent.

**November helps:** damp shows; gutters overflow in rain; heating is on, so feel every radiator; knotweed shows as dead brown canes.

### Outside

| Look at | Sign of trouble | Lever |
|---|---|---|
| Roof, through binoculars | Slipped or missing tiles, sagging ridge, heavy moss, cracked flashing | Re-roof or repair cost |
| Chimney | Leaning, crumbling mortar, cracked top | Rebuild or remove |
| Gutters and downpipes | Drips, green staining, leaking joints | Easy to price; points to damp inside |
| Walls | Stepped cracks from window and door corners; cracks wider than about 3 mm; bulging | Movement: structural engineer; lenders may hold back money |
| 1930s cavity walls | Horizontal cracks in the mortar every few courses | Rusting wall ties: known, priced job |
| Damp-proof course | Soil or patio less than 15 cm below it; blocked air bricks | Damp risk |
| Drains | Lift a manhole cover if allowed: standing water, smell, roots | CCTV survey; repair |
| Retaining walls and garden | Leaning, bulging, no drainage holes, slope towards the house | Rebuild cost, often large on Sheffield hills |
| Trees | Big trees near the house | Subsidence risk; insurers ask |
| Knotweed | Brown hollow canes in clumps | Treatment plan and lender rules; can stop a sale |
| Windows and doors | Misted double glazing, rot, no FENSA etching on the glass | Replacement cost |
| Street | Neighbours' extensions, upkeep, parking, noise | Precedent; resale |

### Inside

| Look at | Sign of trouble | Lever |
|---|---|---|
| Damp | Tide marks, salt bloom, high meter readings low on walls, black mould, musty smell | Cause first, then repair |
| Signs of hiding | Fresh paint on one patch, rugs or furniture in odd spots, dehumidifier, air freshener, windows open in winter | Look under and behind; ask |
| Ceilings | Stains under the bathroom, cracks, sagging | Leaks; old lath and plaster |
| Floors | Marble rolls; floors bounce | Movement or rotten joists |
| Doors | Sticking or cut to fit | Movement |
| Bathroom and kitchen | Cracked or black grout, failed silicone, soft floor by bath or WC, leaks under sinks | Soft floor means rot |
| Electrics | Old fuse box with wire fuses, no RCDs, tester faults, round-pin or surface wiring | Rewire: large, known cost |
| Heating | Boiler age, service book, cold patches on radiators | Replacement; power flush |
| Water | Weak pressure with two taps; dull grey pipe at the stopcock (lead, pre-1970) | Replace lead supply pipe |
| Loft | Thin insulation, daylight, water stains, height joist to ridge (needs about 2.2 m to convert), trussed rafters (W-shaped) | Trusses or a low roof make a conversion much dearer |
| Pre-2000 materials | Artex ceilings, old floor tiles, cement-sheet roofs on garages and sheds | Possible asbestos: test before works |
| Size | Measure every room | Own floor area against the listing's |
| Layout | Where the front door opens; load-bearing walls in the way | Hall rule; cost of steels |

### Standard questions (agent and seller)
1. When first listed? Price changes? Another agent before?
2. How many viewings? Second viewings? Offers? Sale fallen through, and why?
3. Why selling? Found somewhere? Deadline? Empty? Probate?
4. What work done, when, by whom? Certificates, guarantees, building-control sign-off?
5. Insurance claims, neighbour disputes, shared drives or rights of way?
6. What does the sale include?
7. In writing: floorplan, EPC, tenure, council tax band, ground rent and lease terms (material information, Parts A–C).

## 4. The offer pack (pricing rules)

- Offer = value from comparables − cost of faults − a discount for risk.
- Open below that, with the reasoning written down.
- Rise in shrinking steps; set a date for each offer to lapse.
- Ask for the listing to come down when the offer is accepted.
- The agent's fee is roughly 1% of price: £10k off costs the agent about £100.
- Offer before Christmas, while the seller's other choice is a spring relaunch.
- One offer at a time.
- Never tell the agent budget, deposit, ceiling or which house you prefer. Never use death notices or obituaries.

## 6. What's special about each house

**Asking price per m² (worked, 19 Nov):** Falmouth £210k / 108 m² = £1,944; at 94 m² = £2,245 (wave 2 recomputed £2,234). Welby £265k / 60 m² = £4,417. Lees Hall £290k / 69 m² = £4,203. Helmton £265k / 109 m² = £2,431 (**superseded**: 109 m² was No. 25's; No. 17 is 73 m², £3,630/m²). Compare only with the same type nearby; bungalows and detached houses carry a land premium.

### falmouth — 34 Falmouth Road, S7 2DG
- Hall: the front door opens into the living room. Check whether a lobby could be partitioned off. If not, stop here.
- Floor area: listing 1,163 sq ft, Hunters on the phone 1,007 sq ft, a 13% gap. Find the EPC figure and measure. A 13% smaller house is worth about 13% less.
- Tenure: leasehold. Get the ground rent and its terms.
- Area: Nether Edge conservation area or Article 4? Either limits extensions.
- Effort: free checks only. The margin is already negative.

### welby — 50 Welby Place, S8 9DB
- Seller's floor: bought for £242k in November 2021; index to today. NatWest charge May 2026. Recent borrowing probably sets the seller's floor. Never mention it to the agent.
- Also to let? (Wave 2 ruled: for sale only.)
- Drains under the plot: Yorkshire Water sewer map. Building over a public sewer needs consent and can block an extension. CCTV survey before offering.
- Loft: a 1989–90 bungalow almost certainly has trussed rafters, so going up costs more.
- Planning: Class AA permitted development covers houses built July 1948 to March 2018. Confirm not in a conservation area.
- Workshop: is the roof cement sheet (possible asbestos)?
- Site: steep site, retaining walls, one-small-car parking. Each cuts resale value.

### leeshall — 57 Lees Hall Road, S8 9JJ
- Tenure: most of the street is leasehold. The £7 title register settles it, and shows whether a probate sale is likely.
- Extension: planning and building-control records. Without sign-off, it's a lever.
- Floor area: the EPC's 69 m² may leave out the extension. Measure.
- 1930s risks: wall ties, lead water pipe, old wiring.
- Loft: hipped roof means hip-to-gable, which costs more.
- Garden: rear slope with retaining walls.
- Comparables: No. 26 £275k, No. 117 £268k, No. 105 £255k. A rival semi went Sold STC at about £250k.

### cawthorne — 6 Cawthorne Grove, S8 0ND
- Least known; needs full desk research.
- Price: guide tops out at the £325k ceiling. Start well below the bottom of the range.
- "Another viewing may follow" is a pressure line. Ignore it.
- Flood: Millhouses lies along the River Sheaf, which flooded in 2007. Check maps and insurance cost.

### underwood — 38 Underwood Road, S8 8TH
- Cheapest. "Offers around" invites a lower figure.
- The agent meets you there: ask about the seller's position.
- Unknown: hall, type and condition. Price every fault; works are where the money goes.

### helmton — 17 Helmton Drive, S8 8QN
- The seller shows it: best chance on the day to learn motive, timing and onward move. Ask open questions; say little.
- Tenure: confirm freehold. (Wave 2 ruled freehold; the 136-year lease was No. 25's.)
- Price: "at about 109 m² it's cheap per m²" — **superseded**: 73 m², £3,630/m², already done up (wave 2).
- "Offers over" is the agent's anchor. Ignore it.

### hollythorpe — 51 Hollythorpe Road, S8 9NF
