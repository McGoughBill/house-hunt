## At every house

- **Say:** you're a first-time buyer with no chain and an agreement in principle.
- **Don't say:** any figure for your budget, deposit or mortgage, or which house you prefer. Don't quote the research below: the past prices, days unsold and rival listings are your levers for the written offer, not for the doorstep.
- **Ask (the usual):**
  - How long has it been on the market? Any price changes?
  - Has it had any offers, and has a sale fallen through?
  - Why are the owners selling? Is the house empty?
  - Could you email the floorplan?
- **Ask (what I couldn't check online; the sites block automated checks).** Ask the agent for these in writing; they're "material information" the agent should hold:
  - **Energy certificate:** a copy, with its floor area and date.
  - **Flood:** any flooding, and any flood-related insurance claim or excess.
  - **Ground:** any mining report, subsidence, movement or heave claim.
  - **Knotweed:** any on or next to the plot, and any treatment plan.
  - **Trees:** any tree preservation orders on or beside the plot.
  - **Planning:** any applications, refusals or extensions, and building-control completion certificates for any work.
  - **Area rules:** is it in a conservation area or under an Article 4 direction?
  - **Certificates:** FENSA for windows, Gas Safe registration for the boiler, and the last electrical report.
- **Look at:** whether the front door opens into a hall, the room to extend, the slope, damp, cracks and fresh paint. Measure every room.
- **Decline the agent's mortgage broker.** Staves' broker, Onn Financial (Fiona), has texted you. You're using L&C.

- **Weaknesses:** the fit uses only 6 bungalow sales, and only 1 of them is under 4 years old. EPCs or listings confirm only Nos 45 and 50 as bungalows; the rest are probable. Derbyshire Lane's bungalows are 1930s ones on bigger plots. The index covers all detached houses in Sheffield, not bungalows alone. Condition is unknown for every sale.

That's market value, not your price. Your extension plan loses money above about a £220k purchase, so your walk-away should come from the plan: £235k still stands, and the seller has firmer ground to refuse it than the old notes implied.
